# ISO2018Usability

ISO 9241-11:2018 Ergonomics of Human-System Interaction — Part 11: Usability: Definitions and Concepts
International Organization for Standardization (2018)
Geneva: ISO.
URL: https://www.iso.org/standard/63500.html

## Key Definitions

- **Usability**: The extent to which a system, product, or service can be used by specified users to achieve specified goals with **effectiveness**, **efficiency**, and **satisfaction** in a **specified context of use**.
- **Effectiveness**: The accuracy and completeness with which users achieve specified goals.
- **Efficiency**: The resources expended (e.g., time, mental effort, physical steps) in relation to the accuracy and completeness with which users achieve goals.
- **Satisfaction**: The extent to which the user's physical, cognitive, and emotional responses that result from the use of a system, product, or service meet the user's needs and expectations.
- **Context of Use**: Users, tasks, equipment (hardware, software and materials), and the physical and social environments in which a product is used.

## Scope and Application

ISO 9241-11:2018 provides a framework for evaluating the usability of any interactive system, product, or service. It applies to both physical and digital systems, including government websites, public service portals, and emergency information systems.

Usability is defined in terms of three core properties:
1. **Effectiveness** — Can users achieve their goals accurately and completely?
2. **Efficiency** — How much effort (time, clicks, cognitive load) is required to complete goals?
3. **Satisfaction** — Do users feel satisfied with the interaction experience?

These three properties must be evaluated **in a specified context of use**, which includes the intended user population, tasks, environment, and the digital platform being evaluated.

## Relevance to Government Portals and Emergency Services

For digital government portals—particularly emergency services such as fire departments—usability requirements are especially critical. Citizens must be able to:
- Locate emergency contact numbers (effectiveness)
- Complete contact or reporting actions within minimum time and steps (efficiency)
- Feel assured and confident after submitting a report or finding needed information (satisfaction)

Usability failures in emergency public service portals can have direct real-world consequences when users cannot achieve their goals quickly during crisis situations.
