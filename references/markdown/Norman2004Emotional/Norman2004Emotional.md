# Norman2004Emotional

Emotional Design: Why We Love (or Hate) Everyday Things
Donald A. Norman (2004)
Basic Books, New York.

Key Concepts:
Emotional processing occurs at three distinct levels in human-computer interaction:
1. Visceral Level: Immediate, automatic, pre-conscious emotional and sensory reaction to visual aesthetics, colors, typography, shapes, and overall look and feel. In stressful emergency situations, visceral appeal provides calm, reassurance, and clarity.
2. Behavioral Level: Functionality, performance, usability, speed, and ease of use during interaction. It is about how well the system performs, how easy it is to accomplish goals, clear feedback, and minimizing cognitive friction.
3. Reflective Level: Conscious thought, contemplation, pride, trust, and post-interaction evaluation. Users reflect on their overall experience, building lasting confidence and credibility in public emergency services.

When users face crises and emergencies, high cognitive load and emotional panic narrow attention. Interfaces must balance attractive aesthetics with crystal-clear affordances and immediate actionable buttons.
